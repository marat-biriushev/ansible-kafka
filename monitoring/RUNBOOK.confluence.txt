h1. Kafka runbook

One section per alert rule. Section names are the Grafana rule uids without the environment prefix: Grafana rule {{int-kafka-test-jmx-down}} and {{int-kafka-prod-jmx-down}} both point at section {{jmx-down}}. Each section also names the matching alert in {{kafka-alerts.yml}} (Prometheus/vmalert).

Publish this page in Confluence and set each rule's *Runbook URL* to the page address plus the section anchor. Confluence Data Center builds the anchor from the page title and the heading with spaces removed: page "Kafka Runbook", heading {{jmx-down}} gives {{#KafkaRunbook-jmx-down}}. Click one heading link on the published page to confirm the format.

Shell setup on any broker, used below:

{code:language=bash}
K=/opt/kafka/bin; C=/etc/kafka/admin.properties; B=$(hostname -f):9091
{code}

Common checks: {{systemctl status kafka}}, {{journalctl -u kafka -n 100}}, {{/var/log/kafka/server.log}}, {{df -h /data}}. Services VM (kafkasvc01): {{docker ps}}, {{docker logs <container> --tail 100}}.

{anchor:node-down}
h2. node-down

*Meaning:* node_exporter (port 9100) does not answer, or fewer hosts than expected are scraped (4 per cluster: kafka01-03 and kafkasvc01). The host is down, node_exporter is stopped, or 9100 is blocked from Prometheus.

*Check:* ping / SSH the host named in the {{node}} label; {{systemctl status node_exporter}}; {{curl -s localhost:9100/metrics | head}}.
*Act:* bring the host or {{node_exporter}} back ({{systemctl start node_exporter}}). For a broker host also see [jmx-down|#jmx-down].

{anchor:jmx-down}
h2. jmx-down

*Meaning:* the broker's JMX exporter (port 7071) does not answer, or fewer than 3 brokers are scraped. The exporter runs *inside the Kafka process* (javaagent), so this means the Kafka broker itself is down, or port 7071 is blocked from Prometheus.

*Check:* {{systemctl status kafka}}, {{journalctl -u kafka -n 100}}, {{curl -s localhost:7071/metrics | head}} on the node.
*Act:* {{systemctl start kafka}}. If it does not start, read {{/var/log/kafka/server.log}}: full disk, {{meta.properties}} mismatch, certificate problems. With one broker down the cluster keeps working (RF 3, min ISR 2): fix it today, not in a panic. Two brokers down: see [offline|#offline].

{anchor:controller}
h2. controller

*Meaning:* the cluster has no active controller (0) or more than one. 0 means the KRaft quorum is lost (2 of 3 nodes down) or no broker metrics are scraped; 2+ should not happen with KRaft (check clocks and network).

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
$K/kafka-metadata-quorum.sh --bootstrap-server $B --command-config $C describe --status
{code}
{{LeaderId}}, {{CurrentVoters}}; {{chronyc tracking}} on all three nodes.
*Act:* bring the brokers back; the quorum needs 2 of 3 controllers.

{anchor:offline}
h2. offline

*Meaning:* partitions without a leader: writes and reads of them have stopped. Usually two or more brokers are down.

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
$K/kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server $B --command-config $C --describe --unavailable-partitions
{code}
*Act:* start the stopped brokers one at a time. Unclean leader election is off on purpose: do not turn it on, it loses data.

{anchor:under-isr}
h2. under-isr

*Meaning:* partitions with fewer than 2 in-sync replicas: producers with {{acks=all}} get {{NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS}}, the applications cannot write.

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
$K/kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server $B --command-config $C --describe --under-min-isr-partitions
{code}
Which broker is missing from the ISR.
*Act:* bring that broker back, see [jmx-down|#jmx-down].

{anchor:under-replicated}
h2. under-replicated

*Meaning:* some replicas are behind the leader. A minute or two during a broker restart or a rolling restart is normal (warning); 5 minutes and more is not (critical): one more failure can stop writes.

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
$K/kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server $B --command-config $C --describe --under-replicated-partitions
{code}
The broker missing from {{Isr}}, its disk ({{df}}, {{iostat -x 5}}), network, GC pauses in {{server.log}}.
*Act:* a broker that was down catches up by itself. If it does not: its disk or network, or it is overloaded ([handler-idle|#handler-idle]).

{anchor:fenced}
h2. fenced

*Meaning:* the controller has fenced a broker: it stopped sending heartbeats (long GC pause, network, overload). A fenced broker serves no partitions.

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
$K/kafka-metadata-quorum.sh --bootstrap-server $B --command-config $C describe --replication
{code}
{{server.log}} on that broker.
*Act:* usually it unfences itself within seconds; if not, {{systemctl restart kafka}} on it.

{anchor:isr-flapping}
h2. isr-flapping

*Meaning:* replicas keep leaving and rejoining the ISR (more than 10 shrinks/expands in 15 minutes on a broker): unstable network between the brokers, GC pauses, or a slow disk.

*Check:* "Shrinking ISR" / "Expanding ISR" in {{server.log}} of the node in the alert; network errors ([net-errors|#net-errors]); disk latency.
*Act:* remove the cause. Raising {{replica.lag.time.max.ms}} hides it, it does not fix it.

{anchor:handler-idle}
h2. handler-idle

*Meaning:* the broker's request handler threads are busy: idle below 40% (warning) or 20% (critical). Latency grows and clients time out.

*Check:* dashboard "Requests per second by type", which topics grew ("Messages in per topic"), CPU and disk of the node.
*Act:* find the client or topic that grew and its owner; spread partitions or add quotas. A one-off batch load may only need waiting.

{anchor:request-latency}
h2. request-latency

*Meaning:* broker-side p99 of Produce or FetchConsumer is above twice its 7-day average.

*Check:* the same node's disk, GC, network, [handler-idle|#handler-idle], under-replicated partitions.
*Act:* as for handler-idle. After a planned load change the baseline follows within days.

{anchor:produce-errors}
h2. produce-errors

*Meaning:* brokers answer produce requests with errors for 5 minutes. During a broker restart a short burst (leader moves) is normal.

*Check:* [under-isr|#under-isr] and [offline|#offline] first; then {{/var/log/kafka/kafka-authorizer.log}} (denied writes: missing ACL), {{RecordTooLargeException}} / message size in {{server.log}} and the dashboard "Rejected bytes per topic".
*Act:* fix the cluster side, or tell the client team (missing ACL, messages over 1 MB).

{anchor:fetch-errors}
h2. fetch-errors

*Meaning:* brokers answer fetch requests with errors for 5 minutes: consumers or followers ask for offsets that no longer exist, partitions that moved, or topics they may not read.

*Check:* {{kafka-authorizer.log}} (denied reads), {{server.log}} ("OffsetOutOfRange", "NotLeaderOrFollower"); right after a restart it clears by itself.
*Act:* tell the client team (missing ACL, wrong offsets); if it follows a broker problem, fix that.

{anchor:heap}
h2. heap

*Meaning:* a broker's JVM heap is above 90% for 10 minutes. G1 normally fills the heap before collecting, so look at the trend after GC, not one peak.

*Check:* dashboard "JVM heap used / max" and "GC time" for the node; long GC pauses in {{server.log}}.
*Act:* if the heap stays full after GC and GC time grows, the heap is too small for the load: raise {{kafka_heap}} in the inventory and run {{playbooks/kafka.yml}} (rolling restart).

{anchor:disk-usage}
h2. disk-usage

*Meaning:* a filesystem is above 80% / 90%; for the Kafka data disk {{/data}}, above 75% (warning) / 85% (critical). At 100% the broker stops.

*Check:* {{df -h /data}}; the largest topics (dashboard "Size on disk per topic", or Kafka UI).
*Act:* lower {{retention.ms}} / {{retention.bytes}} on the large topics, delete test topics, or grow the disk. **Never delete files in {{/data/kafka}} by hand.** Other filesystems: logs ({{/var/log/kafka}} rotates after 14 days), Docker images on kafkasvc01 ({{docker image prune}}).

{anchor:disk-readonly}
h2. disk-readonly

*Meaning:* a filesystem went read-only, usually after disk or storage errors. On {{/data}} the broker stops writing.

*Check:* {{dmesg -T | tail -50}}, {{mount | grep ' ro,'}}.
*Act:* storage / virtualization team; the broker must be stopped ({{systemctl stop kafka}}) before any fsck. The cluster keeps working with two brokers.

{anchor:disk-inodes}
h2. disk-inodes

*Meaning:* a filesystem has used more than 90% of its inodes: no new files even with free space. Kafka creates files per log segment.

*Check:* {{df -i}}; on {{/data}}, the topics with very many small segments.
*Act:* delete unneeded topics; review {{segment.bytes}} / {{segment.ms}} of topics with tiny segments.

{anchor:data-mount}
h2. data-mount

*Meaning:* fewer than three brokers report a {{/data}} filesystem: the data disk is not mounted (Kafka would then write to the OS disk), or node_exporter is not scraped.

*Check:* {{findmnt /data}} on each broker.
*Act:* do *not* start Kafka on a broker without {{/data}} mounted. Mount it ({{mount /data}}, fstab entry), then start Kafka.

{anchor:cpu}
h2. cpu

*Meaning:* a host uses more than 90% CPU for 10 minutes.

*Check:* {{top}}; for a broker also [handler-idle|#handler-idle] and TLS load (many new client connections).
*Act:* find the client that grew; spread the load or add CPU.

{anchor:memory}
h2. memory

*Meaning:* less than 10% of memory is available for 10 minutes. On a broker the page cache is reclaimable and counted as available, so this means real memory pressure.

*Check:* {{free -m}}, {{ps aux --sort=-rss | head}}.
*Act:* a process other than Kafka using memory; on kafkasvc01 one of the containers ({{docker stats}}).

{anchor:net-errors}
h2. net-errors

*Meaning:* more than one network receive/transmit error per second on a host for 5 minutes.

*Check:* {{ip -s link}}, {{ethtool -S <iface>}} on the host.
*Act:* network / virtualization team; it often explains [isr-flapping|#isr-flapping] and [under-replicated|#under-replicated].

{anchor:cert-expiry}
h2. cert-expiry

*Meaning:* a broker certificate expires in under 30 days (warning) or 7 days (critical). An expired certificate stops inter-broker traffic and client connections.

*Act:* self-signed CA (TEST and PROD now): run {{playbooks/kafka.yml}}; it reissues certificates that expire within 30 days and restarts the brokers one by one. Bank CA: put the new files into {{certs/<env>/}} and run {{playbooks/kafka.yml}}.

{anchor:consumer-lag}
h2. consumer-lag

*Meaning:* a consumer group keeps falling behind for 15 minutes (critical), or its lag is above the threshold (warning; one starting value for all groups until the key groups have an agreed SLO).

*Check:* the group in Kafka UI or Redpanda Console: does it have members? are offsets committed? Dashboard "Lag per group and topic" and "Consumption rate per group".
*Act:* tell the team that owns the group: the consumer is stopped, stuck on a message, or too slow (more partitions / consumers).

{anchor:lag-target}
h2. lag-target

*Meaning:* kafka-exporter on kafkasvc01 (port 9308) does not answer: consumer lag is not monitored.

*Check / act:* on kafkasvc01 {{docker logs kafka-exporter --tail 50}}; {{docker compose --project-directory /opt/kafka-exporter up -d}}.

{anchor:schema-target}
h2. schema-target

*Meaning:* Schema Registry metrics (kafkasvc01 port 7071) do not answer: Schema Registry is most likely down, and producers/consumers using Avro/Protobuf schemas fail.

*Check / act:* {{curl -s localhost:8081/subjects}} on kafkasvc01; {{docker logs schema-registry --tail 100}}; {{docker compose --project-directory /opt/schema-registry up -d}}.

{anchor:connect-target}
h2. connect-target

*Meaning:* Kafka Connect metrics (kafkasvc01 port 7072) do not answer: the Connect worker is most likely down and all connectors are stopped.

*Check / act:* {{curl -s localhost:8083/}} on kafkasvc01; {{docker logs kafka-connect --tail 100}}; {{docker compose --project-directory /opt/kafka-connect up -d}}. Connectors resume from their stored offsets.

{anchor:connector-failed}
h2. connector-failed

*Meaning:* a connector or one of its tasks is FAILED (label {{connector}}).

*Check:*
{code:language=bash}
curl -s localhost:8083/connectors/<name>/status | python3 -m json.tool
{code}
on kafkasvc01: the {{trace}} says why (database unreachable, credentials, schema change, missing ACL).
*Act:* fix the cause, then {{curl -X POST 'localhost:8083/connectors/<name>/restart?includeTasks=true&onlyFailed=true'}} (or Restart in Kafka UI / Redpanda Console).

{anchor:connect-errors}
h2. connect-errors

*Meaning:* a connector's tasks failed to convert or transform records in the last 10 minutes; depending on {{errors.tolerance}} they were skipped or sent to the dead letter queue.

*Check:* the connector's {{errors.*}} settings and its log lines ({{docker logs kafka-connect --since 30m | grep <name>}}).
*Act:* tell the team that owns the connector; fix the data or the connector config.
